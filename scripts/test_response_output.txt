     RESPONSE AND OBJECTION TO PETITION FOR APPOINTMENT OF GUARDIAN
============================================================

I. INTRODUCTION

1. COMES NOW, [RESPONDENT NAME], appearing pro se, and submits this Response and Objection to Petition for Appointment of Guardian and Conservator. 

2. I am responding to a Petition for Appointment of Guardian and Conservator filed against me in Nebraska. The petitioner claims I lack capacity to manage my own affairs, but I am fully competent. I respectfully object to the Petition in its entirety and move for its immediate dismissal.

II. RESPONDENT IS FULLY COMPETENT AND THE PETITION IS UNSUPPORTED BY MEDICAL EVIDENCE

3. The strongest argument against this action is that Respondent is fully competent and the petition is unsupported by medical evidence. The Petitioner lacks evidence of incapacity of any kind.

4. Under Neb. Rev. Stat. Sec. 30-2620, a petition for the appointment of a guardian may be filed, but the burden of proof rests entirely upon the Petitioner to demonstrate that the subject of the petition is incapacitated. Petitioner has failed to meet this burden.

5. I have no history of mental illness. There are no medical records, psychological evaluations, or physician statements attached to the Petition that would suggest any cognitive decline or inability to make responsible decisions.

6. The Nebraska Supreme Court established in Guardianship of Garcia v. Garcia, 631 N.W.2d 464 (Neb. 2001), that the appointment of a guardian requires clear and convincing evidence that the individual lacks the capacity to make or communicate responsible decisions concerning their person. Because the Petitioner lacks evidence of incapacity, the standard set forth in Garcia cannot be met.

III. RESPONDENT HAS MANAGED FINANCES INDEPENDENTLY FOR DECADES

7. The petitioner claims I lack capacity to manage my own affairs, which is demonstrably false. I have been managing my finances independently for decades. 

8. Attached hereto as Exhibit A are financial records and related documentation demonstrating that the subject has managed finances independently for decades, maintaining accounts in good standing without any need for intervention or assistance.

9. In Re Guardianship of Hartwig, 656 N.W.2d 268 (Neb. Ct. App. 2003) demonstrates that courts must carefully scrutinize claims of incapacity, particularly when the alleged protected person has a demonstrated history of managing their own affairs and there is no objective financial evidence of waste or dissipation.

IV. THE PETITION IS MOTIVATED BY A FAMILY FINANCIAL DISPUTE

10. The petition was filed by a family member with whom I have had a financial dispute. 

11. Attached hereto as Exhibit B is documentation regarding this disagreement, which establishes that a family financial dispute is the likely motive for the filing of this Petition, rather than any genuine concern for my well-being or capacity.

12. As noted in In re Guardianship of Novacek (Neb. Ct. App. 2022), guardianship proceedings are designed to protect vulnerable individuals, not to serve as a weapon or leverage in collateral family disputes. The Petitioner is attempting to use the probate court to gain control over my finances due to our ongoing disagreement.

V. MOTION TO DISMISS

13. Pursuant to Neb. Rev. Stat. Sec. 30-2627, the court shall conduct a hearing on the petition. At or before such hearing, this Petition should be dismissed because it is factually baseless, unsupported by medical evidence, and filed for an improper purpose.

14. Because I am fully competent, have no history of mental illness, and have been managing my finances independently for decades, there is no legal or factual basis for the appointment of a guardian or conservator.

VI. PRAYER FOR RELIEF

WHEREFORE, Respondent respectfully requests that this Court:
A. Deny the Petition for Appointment of Guardian and Conservator in its entirety;
B. Dismiss the Petition with prejudice;
C. Recognize that Respondent is fully competent to manage their own affairs; and
D. Grant such other and further relief as the Court deems just and equitable.
